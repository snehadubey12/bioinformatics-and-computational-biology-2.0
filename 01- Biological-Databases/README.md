# Experiment 1: Study of Biological Databases

## Aim

To explore different biological databases and understand how they are used to retrieve and analyze biological information.

## Introduction

Biological databases are online repositories that store and organize biological data. They provide researchers with easy access to information such as DNA sequences, protein sequences, molecular structures, genes, and biological pathways.

In this experiment, four commonly used databases were explored to understand their applications in bioinformatics, focusing specifically on Cytochrome P450 and central metabolic pathways.

## Databases Explored

| Database | Type of Information | Main Application |
|----------|---------------------|------------------|
| NCBI | Nucleotide/Protein sequences | Sequence retrieval and analysis |
| UniProt | Protein sequences and annotations | Protein characterization |
| KEGG | Genes, enzymes and pathways | Pathway analysis |
| PDB | 3D structures | Structural analysis |

## Procedure

### 1. NCBI 

- Opened the NCBI database.
- Searched for the human "cytochrome P450" sequence.
- Selected a suitable partial protein sequence record.
- Observed the accession number (ATY36036.1) and sequence information (49 aa).
- Examined the available annotations associated with the sequence.

### 2. UniProt

- Accessed the UniProt database.
- Searched for the corresponding protein.
- Examined the reviewed protein sequence for Cytochrome P450 26B1 (CP26B_HUMAN).
- Studied information such as protein function, organism (Homo sapiens), and annotations.

### 3. KEGG

- Opened the KEGG database.
- Searched for the "kreb cycle" biological pathway.
- Studied the associated metabolic module (M00009).
- Observed related genes, enzymes, and pathway network diagrams.

### 4. Protein Data Bank (PDB)

- Accessed the PDB database.
- Searched for the protein "cytochrome p450".
- Examined the available search parameters for 3D structures.
- Observed structural database queries and related autocomplete suggestions.

## Observations

### NCBI 
A partial protein sequence record for human cytochrome P450 was obtained.
- **Accession:** ATY36036.1
- **Sequence Length:** 49 amino acids

### UniProt
Comprehensive protein sequence and functional information were retrieved.
- **UniProt ID:** Q9NR63
- **Protein:** Cytochrome P450 26B1 (CYP26B1)
- **Length:** 512 amino acids

### KEGG
Biological pathways and modular network diagrams were studied.
- **Module ID:** M00009
- **Pathway:** Citrate cycle (TCA cycle, Krebs cycle) / map00020

### PDB
Search parameters for three-dimensional structures of Cytochrome P450 biological macromolecules were examined using the structural database interface.

## Result

The major biological databases were successfully explored, and different types of biological information relating to Cytochrome P450 and the Krebs cycle were retrieved. The purpose and applications of NCBI, UniProt, KEGG, and PDB were understood.

## Conclusion

Biological databases are important resources in bioinformatics research. They provide reliable biological information that can be used for sequence analysis, protein characterization, pathway studies, and structural analysis.
